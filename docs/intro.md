# Your Individual Development Roadmap

An Individual Development Roadmap (IDR) is a short, practical agreement with yourself: **what capability will I improve, how will I practise it, and what proof will show that it is improving?**

This portal gives each cohort member a personal workspace, a shared learning toolkit, and a simple review cadence. It is designed to support real work—not create another form to complete.

:::tip Begin with what is true
Your roadmap is a starting point, not a verdict. Replace any suggested focus with the goals, feedback, and assessment insights that are most relevant to you.
:::

## What a strong IDR contains

| Element | The question it answers | Example evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direction | What change matters now? | A one-sentence outcome statement |
| Practice | What will I repeatedly do? | Notes, a prototype, a presentation, a completed task |
| Feedback | Who will help me improve? | A mentor comment or peer review |
| Reflection | What did I learn and adapt? | A short weekly learning log |
| Review | What is the next best step? | A revised 30-day plan |

## The four-week loop

1. **Choose one capability.** Make it specific enough to practise this month.
2. **Create a useful outcome.** Connect learning to a real problem, task, or audience.
3. **Collect evidence every week.** Small artefacts beat vague memory.
4. **Ask for feedback.** One targeted question produces better feedback than “What do you think?”
5. **Review and reset.** Continue, adjust, or select the next capability.

## A good first outcome statement

Use this sentence stem:

> Over the next 30 days, I will strengthen **[capability]** by **[repeatable practice]**, so that I can **[useful result]**. I will know it is working when **[observable evidence]**.

For example: “Over the next 30 days, I will strengthen stakeholder communication by producing one concise project update each week and asking a colleague to review it. I will know it is working when the update answers the audience’s key decisions without follow-up questions.”
